Line-Following Autonomous Vehicle

Lead Engineer · EK 210 · February–May 2025 · Tested prototype

A client-commissioned vehicle designed to complete a 12 ft path in under 90 seconds while avoiding obstacles and carrying a 250 g load.

Pictures/Robot/Vehicle.jpg

Contribution & Results

As lead engineer, I led a four-person team through the design, prototyping, calibration, and performance testing of an autonomous line-following vehicle for EK 210. I performed all wiring and programming. The final vehicle carried a 250 g payload through six successful course trials, with completion times of 70–86 seconds and a mean of 78.5 seconds.

Systems Engineering

I interviewed the client to define objectives, trade-offs, and feedback, then translated those needs into two design reviews and a final technical report. I managed the development timeline with a Gantt chart, coordinated system integration, and directed iterative prototyping and calibration within a $200 budget.

  • Sensor Architecture: Programmed one Arduino Uno to read three HiLetgo IR sensors, two for line following and one for obstacle detection. An IR remote and TSOP38238 receiver provided touchless activation. Two L298 drivers controlled four 6 V DC motors using differential steering.
  • Measured Performance: Recorded zero errors across 60 obstacle-detection trials covering white, black, and cardboard targets, with 20 trials per target type. Separate distance tests showed 100% detection at 10 cm for flat targets, but 50% for angled/chamfered targets.
  • Manufacturing: Designed a PLA chassis and laser-cut acrylic motor mounts, using Wago connectors for tool-free wiring connections.

Drivetrain Iteration

The initial vehicle used two Arduino Unos, 28BYJ-48 stepper motors, and ULN2003 drivers. Speed and torque under load drove the switch to DC motors. The final vehicle used one Arduino Uno, two L298 drivers, and four 6 V DC motors.

Testing & Calibration

All six course trials completed successfully. After obstacle-sensor adjustment, the last four trials recorded stopping distances of 10–13 cm. The first two trials stopped closer than the 10 cm objective, making calibration an important part of the iteration.

Line-sensor testing recorded 100% accuracy at 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 cm, and 95% at 2.0 cm, across 20 trials per distance. Obstacle performance depended on distance and target geometry; it was not universally 100% at 10 cm.

Power testing covered one hour of idle operation after two course runs, with power remaining. This test did not establish one hour of continuous driving.

Design Constraints

Objective: Deliver a reliable, low-cost autonomous vehicle that could follow a client-defined path, avoid obstacles, and carry its required payload without exceeding the time limit.

  • Course Length: 12 ft
  • Time Limit: Under 90 seconds
  • Payload: 250 g
  • Recorded Component Cost: $73.94 of a $200 budget
