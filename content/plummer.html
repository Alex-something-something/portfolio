48-Test Bistable-Model Campaign

I developed three specimen concepts for a one-dimensional physical Ising system, which uses bistable buckling beams as two-state mechanical elements. The test campaign provides force measurements for investigating how neighboring beam states affect the response.

I designed the specimens, test matrix, and procedure; operated the Instron 68SC-5; processed the force data; and produced the plots. The 48 tests comprised 32 initial configurations across four models (one legacy and three I developed), 12 repeat tests, two reversed configurations, and two synchronized-camera trials.

Measured forces spanned 0–181 mN, with peak buckling forces of 9–181 mN. I compared peak force immediately before node buckling for configurations with the same immediate neighbors and different next-nearest-neighbor states. This compares buckling thresholds; energy and coupling-coefficient interpretation remains preliminary.

I synchronized camera recordings with the force response to relate beam curvature to applied force. Previous empirical work used curvature measurements; this campaign produced the laboratory’s first direct force dataset for future coupling analysis. The effective coupling coefficient, Jeffective, has not yet been calculated.

I compared ball-bearing, tight-tolerance LEGO-based, and fixed resin-printed joints. The rotating concepts showed scatter and cases where an adjacent beam buckled instead of the loaded beam. My fixed resin-printed joint, installed through a screw-retained slide-in interface, constrained unwanted motion and produced more controlled buckling.

Structural Design & Dynamic Testing

I designed and machined an adapter mounting a physical Ising-model fixture to the shaker for controlled vibration tests of bistable buckling elements. Estimated plate-and-bolt cost fell from $1,170 outsourced to $71.11 in-house ($67.15 plate, $3.96 bolts).

  • Plate trade: Selected a solid 6061-T6 plate over a honeycomb concept: $67.15 versus $159.53 quoted plate cost, simpler in-house machining, and load paths assessable with hand calculations and SolidWorks FEA. Displacement predictions for the separate cases are retained below.
  • Dynamic Fixture Design: Designed an adapter for a physical Ising-model test fixture, predicting a 0.44 mm maximum deflection under a 3 kg load at 20.1 Hz using SolidWorks Dynamic FEA.
  • Fastener analysis: Checked bored Class 8.8 M10 bolts under preload plus the full per-bolt 11 G external load, giving a conservative static tensile-yield FOS of 1.49. Also checked thread shear, plate bearing, and pull-through. This is a mode-specific static check, not an overall dynamic or fatigue FOS at the 20.1 Hz operating frequency.

FEA & Hand-Calculation Comparison

The design review included separate displacement analyses for the plate and the Ising-model fixture. The reported maxima were 0.173 mm and 0.080 mm, respectively, under their modeled load cases. These are distinct from the 0.44 mm dynamic fixture prediction at 3 kg and 20.1 Hz discussed above.

Precision Manufacturing

Generated G-code and machined the 6061-T6 adapter on a Haas VF-2 CNC mill. The finished geometry included an underside chamfer, corner fillets, and 12 drilled holes held to ±0.005 in.

Applied GD&T and worst-case tolerance-stack analysis, calculated ISO 273 clearances for ISO 4017 M10 hardware, and added 0.5 mm edge breaks. I drilled 5 mm axial bores into standard medium-carbon-steel M10 bolts for the required interface, avoiding custom-bolt procurement. Stainless-steel machining attempts failed, so I changed the bolt material and checked the reduced tensile area; the bored bolts and adapter were installed.

I presented a mini design review of the shaker adapter, covering design iterations, finished hardware, and results, as my flash-talk update at a multi-lab research meeting.

Laboratory Commissioning & Apparatus

I supported the operational build-out of a mechanics-of-materials research laboratory by evaluating and procuring equipment, tooling, and supplies against a $3,500 startup budget.

  • High-speed camera mount: Designed, built, and used an adjustable 45 × 45 mm extrusion stand attached to the shaker table with four M6 bolts. A simply supported top-beam model including self-weight predicts approximately 0.107 mm deflection at the 2 kg camera load, or 0.178 mm at a doubled 4 kg design load. These are static predictions, not measured vibration response.
  • Reusable Test Infrastructure: Chose long, modular extrusion members so the stand could be reconfigured for future experiments while staying within the $390 budget.

Source: DSRF Calcs, Class 8.8 M10 sheet. The model uses 5 kg at 11 G, shared equally by four bolts, and conservatively adds the full per-bolt external force to preload. It assumes an M10 × 1.5 tensile area reduced by a 5 mm axial bore (38.35 mm²), 640 MPa bolt yield, and 9.7 mm local bearing thickness. The 5 mm bore and 9.7 mm local bearing thickness match the installed joint. Preload keeps the joint compressed, while bolt and joint stiffness determine how much external tensile load increases bolt tension versus relieves joint compression. The workbook omits that stiffness-sharing calculation and adds the full external load to preload as a conservative static check. The resulting 1.49 FOS is against bolt tensile yield; the preload-only 1.50 FOS describes the initial stress state, not cyclic capacity. Neither ratio establishes overall dynamic performance or fatigue life at 20.1 Hz. Preload scatter, joint separation and fatigue are not quantified in this workbook. All reported failure-mode ratios use nominal properties, not measured bolt strength. Joint load-sharing reference: NASA TM-106943.
