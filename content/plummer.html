Structural Design & Dynamic Testing

I design and manufacture mechanical fixtures for dynamic research experiments, supporting design reviews with analysis, cost comparison, and tolerance-driven fabrication planning. I reduced estimated hardware cost from $1,170 outsourced to $71.11 through in-house fabrication and fastener selection. The in-house cost comprised $3.96 for bolts and $67.15 for the plate.

  • Material Trade Study: Pivoted a shaker-adapter design from an orthotropic honeycomb matrix to a solid 1/4 in 6061-T6 aluminum plate, improving confidence in isotropic failure-mode characterization and reducing manufacturing risk.
  • Dynamic Fixture Design: Designed an adapter for a physical Ising-model test fixture, predicting a 0.44 mm maximum deflection under a 3 kg load at 20.1 Hz using SolidWorks Dynamic FEA.
  • Fastener Analysis: Calculated preload and thread-shear performance for Class 8.8 and stainless hardware, calculating a 21.7 ft·lbf tightening torque to achieve the intended Class 8.8 bolt preload and confirming a 1.49 FOS for 3,710 lbf peak forces at 11 G and a minimum 2.46 FOS for thread, plate-bearing, and pull-through modes.

Precision Manufacturing

Generated G-code and machined the 6061-T6 adapter on a Haas VF-2 CNC mill. The finished geometry included a circular, chopped-end conical chamfer across the underside, corner fillets, and 12 drilled and tapped holes held to ± 0.005 in tolerances. Removing material from the 3.19 kg stock produced a 1.90 kg finished part, a 40.4% stock-to-part mass reduction.

Applied GD&T and worst-case tolerance-stack analysis, calculated ISO 273 clearances for ISO 4017 M10 hardware, and added 0.5 mm edge breaks.

I presented a mini design review of the shaker adapter, covering design iterations, finished hardware, and results, as my flash-talk update at a multi-lab research meeting.

Laboratory Commissioning & Apparatus

I supported the operational build-out of a mechanics-of-materials research laboratory by evaluating and procuring equipment, tooling, and supplies against a $3,500 startup budget.

  • High-Speed Camera Mount: Designed, built, and used an adjustable overhead stand from 45 × 45 mm 80/20 aluminum extrusion. Calculated deflection below 0.1 mm under a conservative load of twice the expected 2.0 kg static camera mass. Rigidity and axial-buckling calculations used a 68 GPa Young’s modulus and 106,217 mm⁴ area moment of inertia; this was calculated performance, not a measured deflection result.
  • Cost-Conscious Design: Prioritized long, modular extrusion stock that could be reused in more stable future apparatus. Balanced a $390 budget, six-foot stock lengths, and freight constraints while preserving 100% material reusability. The stand was an investment in the laboratory’s future equipment-building capability.
  • Metamaterials Research: Iterated resin-printed pins for mechanical Ising models to maintain ± 0.02 mm clearances, using ball bearings to constrain all degrees of freedom except rotation. The modular hardware was designed to transition from one-dimensional to two-dimensional experiments without revising the individual pins.

Ising-Model Testing & Investigation

I independently collect force measurements of approximately 50–120 mN using an Instron 68SC-5 on pin-based physical Ising systems, investigating buckling energy and coupling between neighboring nodes across buckled-up and buckled-down states.

Comparison with the mathematical models is ongoing. The investigation aims to validate or lend support to the current models, identify where they may need adjustment, and guide further “perfect pin” physical Ising designs. More repeatable hardware also supports investigation of a previously observed data anomaly.

  • Implemented acrylic chemistry/soldering dividers, custom hazardous-material fixtures, 5S workstation topology, and foam-shadowed tool drawers.
