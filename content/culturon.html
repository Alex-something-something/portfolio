Deposition Mapping & Characterization

I planned and executed the company’s first full-scale nanoparticle deposition mapping across chamber locations and magnetic configurations.

Using SEM with ImageJ, Olympus DP74 optical microscopy, and direct visual evidence, I analyzed sample plates and racks to characterize deposition patterns, polarity-dependent accumulation, and asymmetric flow behavior. I combined those observations with first-principles reasoning about fluid and electromagnetic interactions to form a working hypothesis for the contamination's cause and spatial pattern.

I presented the working hypothesis, supporting evidence, and resulting contamination-prevention concepts in a technical design review with internal technical leads and the scale-up team.

Particle-Contamination Mitigation Proposals

I used the working contamination hypothesis to propose geometry changes at both the turbomolecular- and roughing-pump ports and at the bottom of the chamber, along with an electron-curtain concept. The proposals connected sample-plate and rack results from SEM with ImageJ and optical microscopy, direct visual evidence, and first-principles reasoning about fluid and electromagnetic interactions.

  • First-principles fluid dynamics, particle-inertia reasoning, and Stokes-number analysis.
  • An electron-curtain particle-control concept informed by electromagnetic first principles and permanent-magnet experiments within the chamber.
  • Mechanical integration, chamber geometry, and contamination-management considerations at both pump interfaces.

The causal explanation remained a working hypothesis, and the prevention concepts were proposals rather than a manufactured or validated contamination-control system. As the final design deliverables of my internship, selected component-level ideas were developed far enough to prepare for fabrication and testing.

Sample-Rack Design & Manufacturing Considerations

I designed multi-configuration sheet-metal sample racks and deposition coupons in Onshape that could be inserted through the reactor ports and mounted without adhesives. The designs supported sampling across horizontal and vertical chamber locations while accounting for fabrication, assembly, and the material and geometrical constraints of the experiments.

I considered conductivity and streamlined geometry along the chamber walls so the samples could act as an analogue for part of the chamber wall.

  • Bend reliefs, minimum flange dimensions, and fabrication tolerances.
  • Insertion through reactor ports, assembly clearances, and adhesive-free mounting.
  • Sample placement across horizontal and vertical chamber locations.
  • Conductivity, wall geometry, and minimizing flow and field disturbance.
