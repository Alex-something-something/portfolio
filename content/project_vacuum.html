Purpose & Contribution

The chamber let the recovery team compare deployment components under reduced pressure before system-level sea-level tests.

Chamber Design & Test Operations

  • Completed Chamber & Seal: Developed a 352 L test vacuum chamber operating at 0.011 atm to evaluate sealed separation charges and avionics at reduced pressure for a rocket targeting 100,000 ft. Designed the door, tube adapter, and size 381 square-cross-section face seal using the Parker O-Ring Handbook. My co-lead fabricated the door and adapter and designed the mounting clamps.
  • Pump Architecture: Used a Robinair 15310 3 CFM pump for faster initial roughing and an Alcatel 2008A medium-vacuum pump for slower evacuation to a deeper vacuum. This staged procedure now reaches the test condition in approximately 30 minutes. The behavior described here is what I observed in our setup: the Alcatel reached a lower final absolute pressure.
  • Test operations: Initiated test ideas, operated the chamber with my co-lead, and iterated the plumbing and evacuation procedure. Integrated-test leadership is documented on the recovery page.
  • Pressure measurement: My co-lead designed the instrumentation, wrote the acquisition script, and recorded and analyzed the data.
  • Material Reuse: Reused pumps and other equipment found in the lab, along with available stock for the adapter and door assembly.

Installed Door, Adapter & Seal

I designed the installed 0.25 in 6061-T6 door using Roark circular-plate calculations for a full-atmospheric differential. The recorded calculation gives 2.12 FOS against yield. My co-lead fabricated the door and adapter and designed the clamps.

I also sized the tube adapter and size 381 face seal. An initial O-ring groove exceeded the gland-fill target; the groove was corrected before the hardware was fabricated and installed. The assembled chamber operates as intended.

Deployment Test Progression & Measurement Limits

I operated dozens of characterization and component trials spanning legacy black-powder charges and CO₂ piercers. These chamber trials evaluated pressure response and reduced-pressure operation; the integrated deployment tests were conducted separately at sea level.

I also operated flight-computer checks and CO₂ component tests while iterating plumbing and gauge configurations.

Integrated deployment results and test leadership are documented on the recovery page; the CO₂ page covers the piercer sizing and pending system test.

The physical gauge and pressure transducer rarely agreed within 0.5 inHg. The stated 0.011 atm starting condition and the trace near 0.18 psi are approximate indications with an unresolved measurement discrepancy; the instrument reference and any offset correction must be established before interpreting the pressure as a precise altitude equivalent.
