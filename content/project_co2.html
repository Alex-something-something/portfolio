Component Firing & Redesign Basis

Two sea-level integrated black-powder tests exposed a failed deployment charge in each test and parachute damage in the second. I helped replace that deployment method with cartridge-piercing hardware; the Catclaw tie-cutting releases remain separate components.

Standalone vinyl-charge trials had appeared repeatable; the integrated results exposed a gap between component trials and the packed subsystem. I authored the integrated vinyl-charge black-powder test SOP and led the second integrated test, including crew briefings and fire/abort decisions. My co-lead led the first test while I was in Australia. Post-test inspection and failure assessment were collaborative with teammates and our team director. End-seal manufacturing variability is the leading working explanation; neighboring-charge damage is a secondary hypothesis inferred afterward. The root cause remains unconfirmed. Integrated-test history and hypotheses.

I sized the mechanism and contributed CAD, drawings, machining, and component testing. All three teammates contributed to the design and fabrication; we iterated one another’s work.

This mechanism is part of the wider separation and recovery subsystem. The overall architecture, parachute-load analysis, redundancy, and vehicle integration are documented on the subsystem page.

Design, Analysis & Manufacturing

  • System Sizing: Calculated a 4.66 psi separation requirement for four nylon shear pins plus the estimated interface friction. Ideal-gas sizing predicted 10.9 g of CO₂ for the modeled free volume, providing analytical margin with a 16 g cartridge.
  • Pressure & Stress Analysis: Calculated maximum pressure and Lamé stresses for the thick-wall piercer housing. The governing analytical result was 2.01 FOS in hoop stress, with 6.31 FOS against internal-thread shear.
  • Manufacturing: Produced Fusion 360 CAD, engineering drawings, and machined parts for the piercing mechanism.
  • Testing & Iteration: Tested the charge hardware under reduced pressure and prepared the complete subsystem for an upcoming integrated CO₂ test.

Test Status & Next Steps

I helped initiate and operate the reduced-pressure component tests and am preparing the integrated-test SOP. My co-lead designed the instrumentation, wrote the acquisition script, and analyzed the pressure trace below.

The upcoming test will evaluate the piercers in the full recovery assembly, including shear pins, parachutes, lines, release devices, and wiring. See the recovery page for the architecture and integrated-test history.

For equal internal pressure, lower ambient pressure increases the differential across the nosecone interface. Released gas mass alone does not establish that internal pressure: temperature, leakage, free volume, and release timing also govern deployment.
