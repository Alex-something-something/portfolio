Project Scope & Leadership

I lead BURPG’s development of an aerodynamic downcomer fairing for Argo. The fairing encloses the external propellant downcomer and adjacent wiring to reduce drag and protect the routed hardware from the flight environment.

I use set-based design to compare multiple candidate leading-edge, midsection, and boattail profiles against the previous Icarus fairing before final selection. Selection criteria include aerodynamic performance, downcomer and wiring clearance, bolted attachment behavior, composite manufacturability, surface finish, and cost.

Current Status

I am developing the current flow model in Ansys CFD and continuing adjoint CFD work to refine the fairing geometry within packaging, mounting, and manufacturing constraints. I am also directing a team member's independent SolidWorks Flow Simulation study and will review the two models for consistency in predicted flow behavior and aerodynamic loads.

The September 2026 conceptual design review established the candidate geometries and manufacturing approaches. CFD comparison, geometry optimization, composite testing, and final design selection remain in progress, so the page does not yet claim final aerodynamic or structural performance.

Aerodynamic Analysis & Validation

  • CAD & Integration: Model candidate fairing profiles and their interfaces with Argo while preserving clearance for the downcomer, wiring, fasteners, and surrounding vehicle structure.
  • Solver Comparison: Develop the current model in Ansys CFD, direct a team member's SolidWorks Flow Simulation study at Mach 2, 2.5, and 2.81, and review the results for consistency before using them for design decisions.
  • Adjoint CFD: Continue geometry optimization while constraining the resulting profile to physically packageable and manufacturable shapes.
  • Structural Analysis: Transfer aerodynamic loading from the validated flow model into structural evaluation of the fairing and its bolted interfaces.
  • Vehicle Performance: Evaluate the effect of fairing drag on the Argo trajectory using OpenRocket.

Composite Manufacturing & Thermal-Mechanical Testing

The current manufacturing study considers carbon-fiber wet layup over 3D-printed or machined foam molds, with attention to curvature, surface finish, dimensional control, material cost, and the aerodynamic effects of protruding fasteners.

I am designing a representative curved carbon-fiber test article with a bolted interface to evaluate whether the laminate locally dimples or sinks around the fasteners. The article will be held under a sustained static load while temperature is increased, creating a deliberately conservative combined thermal-mechanical test of the attachment region.

The test is intended to establish a conservative allowable temperature under load and observe structural behavior as the epoxy approaches softening. It is not presented as a direct material-property measurement, and no test results are claimed yet.

Project context: Aerocover Design Doc and Argo Aerocover CoDR, September 3, 2026. Candidate concepts and analysis plans are preliminary.
