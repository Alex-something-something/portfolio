Design Brief & Constraints

BURPG’s Hybrid Introductory Project challenged new members to calculate, design, manufacture, and test a nozzle and launch computer that maximized predicted thrust while minimizing mass within fixed chamber pressure, mass-flow-rate, maximum-diameter, and 45,000 ft design-altitude constraints.

Contribution & Results

As my first BURPG project, I led a five-person team to design, optimize, manufacture, and physically hotfire a parabolic hybrid rocket nozzle. The final geometry reduced mass by 73% relative to my initial design and increased predicted thrust by 5%. Analysis predicted 7.86 lbf at the 45,000 ft design condition and 2.65 lbf at sea level. Mechanical stress analysis gave a calculated 7.8 FOS under modeled mechanical loads.

The ABS-like resin nozzle survived an approximately two-second hotfire. A load-cell malfunction prevented usable thrust-data collection, so the thrust improvement remains a prediction rather than a measured result.

Role & Technical Scope

Role: Lead Engineer

I directed the project through three iterative design reviews, coordinating the subteams responsible for the test fire computer, engineering drawings, and FEA. I coordinated cross-team communication and intermediate milestones to keep design reviews and testing on schedule.

  • Nozzle Design & CAD: Modeled an 80%-length Rao parabolic de Laval nozzle in SolidWorks using isentropic-flow equations, G.V.R. Rao’s contour method, Rocket Propulsion Elements, and ANSI standards.
  • FEA & Structural Optimization: Learned and troubleshot SolidWorks FEA from scratch, then assessed stresses using sourced ABS-like resin yield strength, density, Poisson’s ratio, and elastic modulus.
  • Manufacturing: Prepared the single-use nozzle for Formlabs SLA printing in ABS-like resin.

Contour Development

I selected an 80%-length parabolic de Laval contour to retain the performance of a longer conical nozzle while reducing axial length. The converging-diverging geometry chokes the flow at the throat and expands it through the diverging section to produce thrust.

I calculated the area ratio and candidate nozzle lengths, then constructed the diverging contour from a three-point Bézier curve using G.V.R. Rao’s geometric method. Circular arcs define the converging and throat regions.

Structural Analysis

I initially reduced wall thickness until the modeled geometry retained a 2.0 yield FOS. A design review identified fragility risks during print removal, handling, and assembly, so I strengthened the outer contour. The final SolidWorks analysis calculated a 7.8 yield FOS under the modeled mechanical pressure loads.

The analysis used pressure regions for the chamber, throat, diverging surface, and external environment. Bolt fixtures represented the interface with the mounting adapter. Material inputs were sourced for the selected ABS-like resin.

Testing & Data Acquisition

Hotfire: The nozzle survived an approximately two-second firing. The test demonstrated successful physical operation of the printed hardware, but it did not validate the calculated thrust or the full analytical margin.

Launch Computer: I led the team responsible for designing, soldering, and programming the launch computer used to actuate the test. The measurement architecture used LabJack acquisition and a load cell, but a load-cell malfunction on the day of the test prevented usable thrust data.
