Contribution & Results

As my first project with BURPG in Fall 2024, I led a five-person team to design, optimize, and physically hotfire a parabolic hybrid rocket nozzle with a 7.86 lbf design thrust. Relative to my initial design, the final geometry reduced mass by 73% and increased predicted thrust by 5%. Mechanical stress analysis gave a calculated 7.8 FOS under mechanical loads alone. The nozzle survived an approximately two-second hotfire, but a load-cell malfunction prevented thrust-data collection.

Experience & Skills

Role: Overall and Design Lead

I directed the project through three iterative design reviews, coordinating the subteams responsible for the test fire computer, engineering drawings, and FEA. I coordinated cross-team communication and intermediate milestones to keep design reviews and testing on schedule.

  • Nozzle Design & CAD: Modeled an 80%-length Rao parabolic de Laval nozzle in SolidWorks using isentropic-flow equations, G.V.R. Rao’s contour methods, Rocket Propulsion Elements, and ANSI standards. Mapped a Bézier curve across three points for the diverging section and used circular curves for the converging and throat sections.
  • FEA & Structural Optimization: Assessed nozzle stresses using SolidWorks FEA, which I learned and troubleshooted from scratch. Initially optimized the contour to a 2.0 FOS to aggressively cut weight, but after a design review raised manufacturing and assembly fragility concerns, I thickened the geometry to a final 7.8 FOS in yield under the modeled mechanical loads. Thermal effects were not included.
  • Manufacturing: The nozzle was manufactured using a Formlabs SLA printer in ABS-like resin, intended for a single-use hotfire.

Testing & Validation

Hotfire & Data Acquisition: The system fired successfully for approximately two seconds. A load-cell malfunction on the day of the test prevented collection of usable thrust data. The 5% thrust improvement therefore remains a prediction, not a measured result.

Launch Computer: I led the team responsible for designing, soldering, and programming the launch computer used to actuate the test. The planned measurement setup included LabJack acquisition and a load cell.

Motivation & Constraints

Objective: The Hybrid Introductory Project (HIP) is BURPG's hands-on trial for new members to learn compressible flow, nozzle design, and FEA. The goal was to calculate, design, and build a nozzle and test computer that maximized thrust and minimized weight.

  • Optimization Target: 45,000 feet
  • Design Constraints: Strict parameters for optimization pressure, mass flow rate, and maximum diameter.
