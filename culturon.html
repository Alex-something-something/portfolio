Sample-Rack Design & Manufacturing Considerations

I designed multi-configuration sheet-metal sample racks and deposition coupons in Onshape for insertion into the vacuum reactor. The designs supported sampling across horizontal and vertical chamber locations while accounting for fabrication, assembly, and the material and geometrical constraints of the experiments.

I considered conductivity and streamlined geometry along the chamber walls so that the samples could act as an analogue for part of the chamber wall. The reactor designer prohibited adhesives, making mounting and material choices important design constraints.

  • Bend reliefs, minimum flange dimensions, and fabrication tolerances.
  • Reactor-port access, assembly clearances, and mounting without adhesives.
  • Sample placement across horizontal and vertical chamber locations.
  • Conductivity, wall geometry, and minimizing flow and field disturbance.

Deposition Mapping & Characterization

I planned and executed the company’s first full-scale nanoparticle deposition mapping across chamber locations and magnetic configurations.

Using SEM, Olympus DP74 optical microscopy, and ImageJ, I characterized deposition patterns and investigated polarity-dependent accumulation and asymmetric flow behavior. This work provided an experimental basis for evaluating contamination mechanisms and potential mitigation approaches.

Mechanism Investigation

I compared observations with published plasma-polymer nanoparticle research and used first-principles fluid dynamics to investigate particle transport and asymmetric gas-flow behavior. My permanent-magnetic-field experiments within the chamber inspired electromagnetic particle-control concepts.

I presented experimental findings and proposed mitigation approaches to internal technical leads and the scale-up team.

Pre-Pump Collection Concept

I developed a pre-pump particle-collection concept intended to intercept nanoparticle byproducts before they reached high-vacuum turbomolecular pumps.

  • First-principles fluid dynamics, particle-inertia reasoning, and Stokes-number analysis.
  • Electromagnetic particle-control concepts inspired by permanent-magnet experiments within the chamber.
  • Mechanical integration and contamination-management considerations.

The complete collection system remained at the concept stage. It was not fully detailed in CAD, manufactured, integrated, or validated as an operating system. As the final design deliverables of my internship, selected component-level concepts were developed far enough to prepare for fabrication and testing.
